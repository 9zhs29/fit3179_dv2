Made in Australia

Hollywood blockbusters, homegrown dramas and streaming series spent a record $2.7 billion making screen stories here last year. Where did that money go, who paid for it, and where do the cameras actually roll?

By Ewan Chong · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · October 2026

SCENE 1 — THE MONEY

Most of the money is now foreign

In 2024/25, drama production in Australia (feature films, TV and streaming series) hit $2.7 billion, the highest figure Screen Australia has ever recorded.

But for every $100 spent, about $60 came from international productions: overseas studios that shoot here or do their visual effects here. Australian stories made up the other $40.

Each square = $1 out of every $100 spent. Hover for dollar amounts.

SCENE 2 — BOOM, BUST, BOOM

A rollercoaster decade

Spending has swung wildly. Australia's early COVID control made it a "safe haven" for Hollywood in 2020/21. Then the 2023 US writers' and actors' strikes stopped many productions, and 2023/24 dropped to $1.7 billion. International shoots drove the record rebound in 2024/25.

Source: Screen Australia, Drama Reports 2022/23, 2023/24, 2024/25. Spend is counted in the financial year production started. 2024/25 international figure = total minus Australian titles.

SCENE 3 — THE STATE RACE

Queensland steals the crown

For years, New South Wales was the undisputed home of Australian screen production. In 2024/25, Queensland overtook it for the first time in this period, thanks to blockbusters filmed at the Gold Coast's studios.

A one-year swing

NSW's share of national spending fell from about half to under a third in a single year. Queensland's doubled. Big international shoots move from state to state, so one or two films can reshape the whole map.

ACT, NT and Tasmania are reported together by Screen Australia to protect confidentiality.

SCENE 4 — ON THE MAP

Per person, Queensland is the screen state

Dividing each state's production spend by its population shows how much the industry matters locally. Queensland's $925 million works out to about $161 for every resident, well ahead of Victoria and NSW.

Sources: Screen Australia Drama Reports; Australian Bureau of Statistics, National, state and territory population (March 2026).

SCENE 6 — GOING GLOBAL

Our co-production partners

An official co-production is a film or series made under a treaty between Australia and another country, so it counts as "local" in both. Since 1986, 230 titles have been made this way. Most partners are Commonwealth countries or in Europe.

Source: Screen Australia, Co-production trends (as at 30 June 2026).
